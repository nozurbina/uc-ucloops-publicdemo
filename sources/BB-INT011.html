BB-INT011-t1Interviewer

How did you first encounter BorderBlend?

BB-INT011-t2Thomas

Food truck festival last summer — the big one in the river valley. There were probably forty trucks there. I went with my brother. We tried a bunch of things and the BorderBlend brisket taco was the standout. Not a close call. We went back to the truck twice that day.

BB-INT011-t3Interviewer

What was it about it?

BB-INT011-t4Thomas

The beef. The smoking technique is real — you can taste the difference from something that's just been braised and called brisket. And the combination. The salsa verde cuts through the fat. It's actually thought through. I'm not a chef but I understand when something has been worked on.

BB-INT011-t5Interviewer

After the festival, how did you find them regularly?

BB-INT011-t6Thomas

I looked them up online — found their website, found the truck locations. There's one about a ten-minute drive from my house that runs on Saturday mornings. I started going. It became a habit.

BB-INT011-t7Interviewer

How often do you go now?

BB-INT011-t8Thomas

Two to three times a month. Saturday lunch mostly. Sometimes I'll stop by on a weekday if I'm near a truck during a work site visit.

BB-INT011-t9Interviewer

You don't have a loyalty programme account. Is that deliberate?

BB-INT011-t10Thomas

I signed up for a loyalty card for a coffee place years ago and never used the points because the interface to check them was annoying. I've been sceptical of loyalty programmes since. Unless it's actually easy — like, it tracks automatically and gives me something real without me having to manage it — I'm not interested in another app or card to keep track of.

BB-INT011-t11Interviewer

What would "actually easy" look like?

BB-INT011-t12Thomas

You scan something when you pay, points accumulate, you get notified when you have enough for something. Done. No separate app download. No account creation before you can buy. Just — it works in the background. Some coffee chains do this now. If BorderBlend had something like that, I'd probably use it passively without changing anything about how I visit.

BB-INT011-t13Interviewer

Is there anything about the brand that puts you off or creates friction?

BB-INT011-t14Thomas

The location schedule. I know my Saturday truck is usually there, but I've shown up twice and it wasn't there with no notice. I don't follow their social media so I wouldn't see an Instagram story about a schedule change. I just showed up and found an empty spot. I'd probably appreciate a simple text alert — "the truck won't be at [location] this Saturday" — if I opted into something like that. No content, no promotion. Just operational information.

BB-INT011-t15Interviewer

Do they have anything like that?

BB-INT011-t16Thomas

Not that I know of. Maybe on the app. I haven't looked closely.

BB-INT011-t17Interviewer

What about the food itself — are there items besides the brisket you've explored?

BB-INT011-t18Thomas

The plant-based one surprised me. I'm not vegetarian but a friend is, and she came with me once. I tried the plant-based option out of curiosity and it was genuinely good — it didn't feel like a compromise item. That changed my impression of the brand. A food truck that puts the same thought into the plant-based option as the signature item is taking it seriously.

BB-INT011-t19Interviewer

Would you recommend the brand to someone?

BB-INT011-t20Thomas

I have. Multiple people at work. My sister-in-law. Usually I describe it the way I experienced it — "you won't expect it to be that good." Low expectations, good outcome. That's actually a reliable recommendation.

BB-INT011-t21Interviewer

Any last thoughts?

BB-INT011-t22Thomas

Just that the basics are right. Good food, reasonable price, reliable enough. The small friction points — schedule reliability, the loyalty thing — don't outweigh the core product. But if they were fixed, they'd probably make me a more consistent customer.
