## The brand
- **BorderBlend** — a Canadian food-truck brand. Founded ~2019 (a *new, growing challenger*, ~6–7 years
  old by 2026). **27 trucks** across Canada (i.e. "20–30 trucks" scale — NOT a huge multinational).
- **HQ:** Toronto. **Markets:** Toronto (largest cluster), Montreal (French/Quebec), Vancouver,
  Calgary, Edmonton (Prairies). Franchise model — individual operators own/run trucks; HQ ("the brand
  team") provides menu, supply, marketing kits, portal, support.
- **Positioning:** *"leveled-up street food."* Not greasy-spoon, not a celebrity-chef pop-up. Mexican-
  rooted. Sustainability baked in low-key: no plastic cutlery ("tacos are hand food — if you need a
  fork, bring your own"), minimal waste.
- **Domain:** borderblend.ca. Consumer **app** (iOS/Android) + web ordering. **Loyalty programme**
  (points; currently opaque/underused). Instagram + TikTok (national account + local truck accounts;
  local accounts inconsistently maintained). **Franchisee portal** + franchisee **Slack**. Active on
  the **festival / event circuit** (a major discovery channel).

## The central strategic tension — FUSION vs TRADITIONAL
BorderBlend does **both** traditional northern-Mexican tacos **and** fusion, and is *still deciding how
hard to lean into fusion.* This is the brand's live strategy question and should surface across the
material (consumers, franchisees, market report, insights).
- **Traditional side:** carne asada, barbacoa, pollo (chicken); corn & flour tortillas; the
  **salsa verde** is a signature that regulars put on everything.
- **Fusion side (the differentiator):**
  - **Smoked Brisket taco** — the **HERO / signature** item. *Actually* smoked brisket (real smoker),
    Mexican preparation. "Closest thing to real BBQ at a taco truck." Drives search + social + loyalty.
  - **Korean-style chicken taco** — gochujang, pickled slaw. "Korean-Mexican combo that makes sense."
  - **Plant-based option** — over-delivers; converts skeptical vegetarians ("actually good? at a
    brisket taco truck??").
- Fusion credibility line consumers use: *"it doesn't feel like a gimmick — the flavours make sense."*
- Brand-voice rule: **no "authentic" claims and no superlatives** (it's a fusion brand, so "most
  authentic Mexican" is off-limits and has caused a compliance flag).
- **Seasonal:** limited-time menus, e.g. **"Fuego Nights"** (summer). Seasonal drops create social/urgency
  moments but also expectation failures when supply can't execute everywhere.

## Known frictions (grounded in the datasets — reuse, don't contradict)
- **Real-time truck status / location** is the single biggest conversion barrier: people can't tell if a
  specific truck is open *now* or where exactly → 35–49% app drop-off; drove-there-and-no-truck stories.
- **App ↔ POS sync** failures; **static app menu ≠ what's actually on the truck board** that day.
- **Allergen info** hard to find (31% app drop-off; a real exclusion for allergy sufferers).
- **Price sensitivity** emerging ("almost the same as a restaurant now").
- **Lines / sellouts** at busy high-volume locations (brisket sold out at 1pm Saturday); execution
  variability at scale.
- **French/Quebec localisation** always arrives late & sometimes uses European-French terms, not fr-CA.
- **Local Instagram dormancy** — some local accounts barely post; "can't tell if you're open."
- **Loyalty programme** opaque; most abandon the account interface.
- HQ content/knowledge gaps (the A–O pain points): no single source of truth for a menu item; fusion
  story explained differently by every franchisee; brand voice is a static PDF; no searchable record of
  past campaigns; franchisee Slack unmonitored; campaigns arrive without brief/rationale; customer
  signals siloed; event circuit undocumented; no approved language for when things go wrong.

## Framing rule (non-negotiable)
BorderBlend is **winning and growing**. Frame every friction as an *obstacle to extending the lead and
scaling further* — never as failure, collapse, or a mopey "nothing works" lament. Franchisees are
invested partners leveling up; consumers are enthusiasts. Real frictions (schedule reliability, app,
French localisation, fusion clarity, brand compliance) are things to solve to *go further/faster*.
