## Brand story

BorderBlend began in Toronto with a chef-founder, Sol Reyes, who kept a backyard smoker running all
weekend and had a standing weakness for the tacos of northern Mexico — carne asada off a hot comal,
slow barbacoa, a salsa verde worth driving for. The two obsessions collided one summer: what if the
patience of real Texas-style smoke met the craft of a proper Mexican taco? The first smoked-brisket
taco sold out of a single truck at a Toronto street festival, and the line kept coming back.

From there the growth followed the crowds. BorderBlend worked the festival and event circuit hard —
letting people discover the food in the moment rather than through advertising — then converted that
momentum into a franchise model. Six-odd years on, that first truck has become 27, run by independent
operators in five cities, still anchored to the same idea: street food, done with more care than the
category expects.

## Vision & mission

**Vision:** To be Canada's defining street-food brand — the one that proved a taco truck can out-craft
the restaurant without losing the joy, the speed, or the street.

**Mission:** We bring people genuinely great food, fast, wherever the crowd is — pairing the roots of
northern-Mexican cooking with real technique (and a real smoker), and giving every operator the tools
to deliver it consistently, truck after truck, city after city.

## Positioning

BorderBlend is **"leveled-up street food"** — Mexican-rooted, blending traditional dishes with
confident fusion. We are deliberately neither the greasy-spoon lunch-wagon nor the here-today
celebrity-chef pop-up: we are the brand that takes the craft seriously and keeps it accessible.

Sustainability sits in the background rather than on a banner. There is no plastic cutlery — tacos are
hand food, and if you need a fork, bring your own — and the operation is built to keep waste low. It's
a stance, quietly held, not a marketing line.

## Menu

**Traditional core.** Carne asada, barbacoa, and pollo (chicken), served on corn or flour tortillas.
The **salsa verde** is a signature — regulars put it on everything, and it does as much to define the
brand as any headline item.

**Fusion (the differentiator).** The **smoked brisket taco is the hero** — actually smoked, in a real
smoker, in a Mexican preparation; the closest thing to true barbecue you'll find at a taco truck, and
the item that drives search, social, and loyalty. Alongside it: a **Korean-style chicken taco**
(gochujang, pickled slaw — the Korean-Mexican combination that simply makes sense) and a
**plant-based option** that over-delivers and routinely converts the skeptics.

**Seasonal drops.** Limited-time menus such as **Fuego Nights** in summer create moments of urgency and
social energy around the trucks.

> **Brand-voice rule:** No "authentic" claims and no superlatives. BorderBlend is a fusion brand — the
> flavours make sense on their own, and the food does the talking. "Most authentic Mexican" is off-limits.

## The strategic question

The live decision is **how hard to lean into fusion versus tradition.** BorderBlend genuinely does both
well: the traditional core and salsa verde earn loyalty and legitimacy, while the smoked brisket and
fusion line are what people search for, post, and travel for. The growth choice ahead is one of
emphasis — how far to push the fusion identity that differentiates us, without loosening the roots that
ground us. It's a question of where to press an advantage, and the brand is choosing deliberately.

## Business & franchise model

The **HQ brand team** in Toronto supplies the platform: menu and recipes, supply chain, marketing kits,
the operator portal, and ongoing support. **Franchisees own and run their trucks** as independent
operators, carrying local knowledge of their city and their regular spots. The **festival and event
circuit** remains a core channel — a way to put the food in front of new crowds and open new markets
before a permanent presence exists.

## Digital & channels

- **App (iOS/Android) + web ordering** — the primary consumer touchpoint for menu, ordering, and finding a truck.
- **Loyalty programme** — points-based, with room to become a much sharper relationship driver.
- **Social** — Instagram and TikTok, run as a national account plus local truck accounts that carry the day-to-day personality of each city.
- **Operator tooling** — a franchisee **portal** and a franchisee **Slack** for coordination, support, and brand updates.

## Audiences

**Consumers.** Late-night foodies (out at night, food-culture-driven, social); business-lunch
professionals (weekday midday, speed and reliability, group orders); everyday 20-somethings
(convenience, value, vibe); and families looking for a genuinely good, easy meal. **Operators.** The
franchisees themselves — invested partners building businesses on the brand and looking to scale.

## Competitive context

BorderBlend competes with independent food trucks, fast-casual Mexican chains, and the growing field of
delivery-only ghost kitchens. Our edge is twofold: a **genuinely smoked hero product** that most of the
category can't match, and **fusion credibility** — flavours that read as considered, not gimmicky. That
combination is what lets a 27-truck challenger keep punching above its size and extending its lead.
