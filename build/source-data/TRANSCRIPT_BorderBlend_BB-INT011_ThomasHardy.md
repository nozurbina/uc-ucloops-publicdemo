## Pre-interview notes

Participant recruited through consumer panel. Discovered BorderBlend about ten months ago at a summer food truck festival in Edmonton. Has since found a truck near his neighbourhood that he visits regularly. Works as a civil engineer. Describes his relationship with food as "practical — I want something good, I don't want to think about it too much." No loyalty programme account; no social media engagement with the brand.

## Transcript

**Interviewer:** How did you first encounter BorderBlend?

**Thomas:** Food truck festival last summer — the big one in the river valley. There were probably forty trucks there. I went with my brother. We tried a bunch of things and the BorderBlend brisket taco was the standout. Not a close call. We went back to the truck twice that day.

**Interviewer:** What was it about it?

**Thomas:** The beef. The smoking technique is real — you can taste the difference from something that's just been braised and called brisket. And the combination. The salsa verde cuts through the fat. It's actually thought through. I'm not a chef but I understand when something has been worked on.

**Interviewer:** After the festival, how did you find them regularly?

**Thomas:** I looked them up online — found their website, found the truck locations. There's one about a ten-minute drive from my house that runs on Saturday mornings. I started going. It became a habit.

**Interviewer:** How often do you go now?

**Thomas:** Two to three times a month. Saturday lunch mostly. Sometimes I'll stop by on a weekday if I'm near a truck during a work site visit.

**Interviewer:** You don't have a loyalty programme account. Is that deliberate?

**Thomas:** I signed up for a loyalty card for a coffee place years ago and never used the points because the interface to check them was annoying. I've been sceptical of loyalty programmes since. Unless it's actually easy — like, it tracks automatically and gives me something real without me having to manage it — I'm not interested in another app or card to keep track of.

**Interviewer:** What would "actually easy" look like?

**Thomas:** You scan something when you pay, points accumulate, you get notified when you have enough for something. Done. No separate app download. No account creation before you can buy. Just — it works in the background. Some coffee chains do this now. If BorderBlend had something like that, I'd probably use it passively without changing anything about how I visit.

**Interviewer:** Is there anything about the brand that puts you off or creates friction?

**Thomas:** The location schedule. I know my Saturday truck is usually there, but I've shown up twice and it wasn't there with no notice. I don't follow their social media so I wouldn't see an Instagram story about a schedule change. I just showed up and found an empty spot. I'd probably appreciate a simple text alert — "the truck won't be at [location] this Saturday" — if I opted into something like that. No content, no promotion. Just operational information.

**Interviewer:** Do they have anything like that?

**Thomas:** Not that I know of. Maybe on the app. I haven't looked closely.

**Interviewer:** What about the food itself — are there items besides the brisket you've explored?

**Thomas:** The plant-based one surprised me. I'm not vegetarian but a friend is, and she came with me once. I tried the plant-based option out of curiosity and it was genuinely good — it didn't feel like a compromise item. That changed my impression of the brand. A food truck that puts the same thought into the plant-based option as the signature item is taking it seriously.

**Interviewer:** Would you recommend the brand to someone?

**Thomas:** I have. Multiple people at work. My sister-in-law. Usually I describe it the way I experienced it — "you won't expect it to be that good." Low expectations, good outcome. That's actually a reliable recommendation.

**Interviewer:** Any last thoughts?

**Thomas:** Just that the basics are right. Good food, reasonable price, reliable enough. The small friction points — schedule reliability, the loyalty thing — don't outweigh the core product. But if they were fixed, they'd probably make me a more consistent customer.

**Interviewer:** Thanks very much.

## Post-interview notes

Discovery path: food truck festival — an event-channel acquisition that the brand doesn't track well (no attribution mechanism at festival contexts). Location schedule alert without requiring social media follow or app download is specific and minimal — essentially an SMS opt-in for operational information. This is a lower bar than the app-based solution Priya and Rafael described, and may represent a broader segment preference. Plant-based item quality noted positively and in context of inclusive hospitality (brought a vegetarian friend, tried it himself) — unexpected advocate for the plant-based line. Loyalty programme friction consistent with the value-driven consumer archetype broadly: passive accumulation is acceptable, active management is not. "You won't expect it to be that good" as a word-of-mouth framing is interesting — low expectations as a discovery advantage. Three-source confirmation of schedule reliability as a friction point (Rafael, Priya, Thomas) — strong candidate for Verified Insight.
