# Grace — Business Lunch Journey (Coordinator Track)

## Stage 1: The Trusted Tip

### Goals:
- Identify a vendor that could level up our team lunches beyond the sad-tray-of-sandwiches default
- Establish whether BorderBlend is even a candidate for group work: do they cater, can they invoice, will they show up
- Protect my own name — no team exposure until I've vetted it personally

### Narrative:
Okay, so here's the thing — I didn't find them the way a normal person finds a taco truck. One of our engineers came back from a downtown offsite going "there's a truck by the LRT, the brisket one, you have to." Lovely. But I'm the person who feeds thirty people, so my brain skips straight past the taco to the checklist: do they cater? Can they invoice? Will they actually show up? So first I pull up borderblend.ca at my desk, notes open, and I start looking for a catering page before I've tasted a single thing. That's the order of operations for me. Always has been.

### Questions:
- Do they do group and catering orders at all, or is this strictly a one-taco-at-a-time operation?
- Can they produce a proper invoice — itemised, GST broken out, our billing name on it?
- Is there a Calgary truck with a regular spot, or is it wherever the wind blows?
- Who do I even contact — head office in Toronto, or the local operator?
- What happens in winter? Because this is Calgary, and February is coming whether they like it or not.

### Problems:
- The website is clearly built for the person buying one taco on their lunch — the group/catering path is not obvious from a first look, and I'm the one arriving with real money
- An enthusiastic tip tells me the food is good; it tells me absolutely nothing about reliability, and reliability is my entire job
- I once had a caterer no-show a 22-person client lunch — VP in the lobby, forty panic pizzas — so every new vendor starts from "prove to me you'll be there"
- No visible answer to the boring-but-critical questions: invoicing, per-head pricing, headcount handling

### Tasks:
- Note the engineer's tip and where the truck parks relative to our office
- Look BorderBlend up on borderblend.ca with my organiser's checklist in hand — catering, invoicing, contact route
- Book myself a solo reconnaissance lunch before the team's name goes anywhere near this

### Sentiment:
Intrigued-but-guarded

### Think/Feel Quote:
"Great, the brisket one — but do they cater, can they invoice, and are they going to actually show up?"

### Alternate Paths:
- The website shows no group path at all and I quietly file BorderBlend under "nice for someone else's lunch" and move on to a documented caterer
- The tip comes with a second endorsement from another exec, which bumps the recon lunch up my calendar
- I can't find a Calgary contact and email head office in Toronto instead, adding a week of back-and-forth

### Channels:
- Exec/colleague recommendation (in person, back from the offsite)
- borderblend.ca (desk research)
- My own vendor-vetting notes/checklist
- Office Slack (where the engineer's tip gets repeated)

## Stage 2: The Solo Vet

### Goals:
- Personally verify the food before any team exposure — I will never put my name on something I haven't eaten myself
- Scout the operation up close: speed, service, how they handle a rush, what sells out and when
- Decide whether the quality justifies the real work of investigating catering, invoicing, and reliability

### Narrative:
So I take my own lunch hour for this — that's the rule, non-negotiable, I've been burned before. I walk over to the truck by the LRT, stand in the line like a civilian, and order the smoked brisket. And, credit where it's due: it's very good. Not sad-tray-of-sandwiches good — actually good, actually smoked. But here's the thing, I'm not just eating, I'm watching. How fast the line moves. How the two of them handle the rush. And I clock that the brisket sells out — early afternoon, gone. Great problem for them. A flashing warning light for me and my thirty people.

### Questions:
- If brisket sells out on an ordinary day, what happens to a booked order for thirty — is my stock reserved or am I gambling?
- The Korean-style chicken, the salsa verde — what's actually in the sauces? Sesame? Nuts? Shared prep?
- Can this two-person window physically serve thirty people in a lunch window, or does group mean a different setup?
- Who runs this truck — is the Calgary operator someone I can build a working relationship with?
- The plant-based option — is it good enough for my vegetarians, or a token?

### Problems:
- The menu board tells me "Korean-style chicken taco, delicious" — it does not tell me what's in the sauce, and I have a colleague with a genuine EpiPen-grade tree-nut allergy, so I cannot guess
- Watching the brisket sell out by early afternoon plants the sellout fear for group orders: "first come" is fine for one taco and a nightmare scenario for a client event
- One truck, one window, one line — nothing about the walk-up experience tells me how they'd handle thirty covers on a schedule
- It's a gorgeous day today; standing here I'm already doing the math on minus twenty-five in February and clients in parkas

### Tasks:
- Take my own lunch hour for the trial run; order the smoked brisket
- Observe the truck as a vendor, not just a meal — throughput, professionalism, sellout timing, how they speak to customers
- Chat briefly with the operator at the window; get a name and confirm they do catering
- Start looking up group orders and catering properly the moment the food clears the bar — which it does

### Sentiment:
Impressed-cautious

### Think/Feel Quote:
"Okay, the food's real. Now show me you can do this thirty times, on a date, in writing."

### Alternate Paths:
- The truck isn't at its spot the day I walk over — and that single empty corner would honestly have ended the evaluation right there
- The food is merely fine, and I keep our current caterer; good-not-great doesn't justify new-vendor risk
- The operator at the window is switched-on and hands me a catering contact directly, skipping a website step I was dreading

### Channels:
- The truck on-site (walk-up, in line, at the window)
- Face-to-face with the local operator
- borderblend.ca (menu double-check from my phone in the line)
- My notes app (vendor observations, sellout time)

## Stage 3: Into the Rotation

### Goals:
- Move from personal endorsement to a first team order without putting my reputation at risk — the casual Friday treat, not the client event
- Get the price up front, per head, so I can have it pre-approved — I'm more sensitive to surprises than to the number
- Establish a working channel with the Calgary operator: availability, menu, headcount, dietary

### Narrative:
Right, so now the real work starts. I go back to borderblend.ca looking for the catering front door, and — it's there, but it's kind of buried, honestly. Built for the one-taco person, not for me booking thirty. I fill out the form, and then the Calgary operator emails back — lovely guy, genuinely — and we sort the actual details over email and one phone call, which I don't mind, I prefer a human for anything that matters. But there were about four steps before I even knew if my date was available. Meanwhile I've sent my form to the team: how many, and who can't eat what. Friday treat first. Low stakes. That's deliberate.

### Questions:
- What's the per-head price, in writing, before I go to finance — not after?
- Which Fridays is the truck actually available, and how far ahead do I need to book?
- Can the menu flex for my two vegetarians, my plant-based person, and my tree-nut colleague?
- Is the form-then-email-then-call dance the permanent process, or is there a faster lane for repeat bookings?
- If Friday goes well, what does the bigger monthly lunch look like — same process or something sturdier?

### Problems:
- The catering path is buried on a site built for the solo buyer — I'm arriving waving money and I have to hunt for the door
- Four steps before I even know if they're available on my date; availability should be the first answer, not the fourth
- No published per-head catering price sheet, so pre-approval with finance means back-and-forth I shouldn't have to do
- My team form comes back with the usual mosaic — vegetarians, one plant-based, one serious allergy — and the public menu gives me nothing ingredient-level to plan against

### Tasks:
- Hunt down the catering/group enquiry form on borderblend.ca and submit it
- Work availability, menu, headcount and pricing out with the Calgary operator over email and one call
- Send the team form: how many, and who can't eat what; collate the answers
- Get the Friday-treat total pre-approved before confirming anything

### Sentiment:
Determined

### Think/Feel Quote:
"I'm on your side here — I want to give you my business. Just make the door easier to find."

### Alternate Paths:
- My preferred Friday is taken and the booking slips two weeks, cooling the team's enthusiasm off the back of my glowing endorsement
- Finance balks at an unconfirmed estimate and I have to go back to the operator for a written number before I can re-submit
- The email thread goes quiet for three days mid-planning and I start, quietly, pricing the better-documented competitor as a fallback

### Channels:
- borderblend.ca (catering enquiry form)
- E-mail with the Calgary franchisee/operator
- Phone (one planning call)
- Office Slack + team dietary form (headcount, who can't eat what)
- Finance/AP system (pre-approval)

## Stage 4: Proving the Operation

### Goals:
- Secure a hard, forwardable, in-writing confirmation: date, arrival time, duration, truck, driver's cell — locked
- Get ingredient-level allergen information in writing that my tree-nut/EpiPen colleague can judge for himself
- Have our stock ring-fenced — reserved, prepped, guaranteed for my count, not first-come
- Run the Friday treat, then the monthly lunch, then the high-stakes client catering in front of executives — and know the winter plan

### Narrative:
Okay, this is my pet peeve, so bear with me. The operator's confirmation was "yep, we'll be there!" — friendly, warm, and completely unforwardable. I am not sending "yep!" to leadership. So I chased it and pinned it down myself: date, arrival 11:30, ninety minutes, truck, driver's cell. Same story with allergens — I emailed, got "let me check with the kitchen," and had to push until it was written and specific, because I don't gamble on somebody's airway. The invoice took a couple of asks too. And then? They came. On time, full headcount, food was a hit, the client event landed beautifully. I just didn't sleep the night before — because I didn't have the paper until I'd extracted it myself.

### Questions:
- Can I get a real booking confirmation as standard — not something I have to chase every single time?
- Is the allergen answer written, ingredient-level, and final — or still "let me check with the kitchen"?
- Is my thirty-person order ring-fenced, or could a good Friday walk-up line eat my brisket?
- Will the invoice arrive as an itemised PDF — GST broken out, billing name, PO reference — without a second ask?
- What exactly is the November-through-March plan — drop-off? indoor setup? Because nobody's queueing at a window at minus twenty-five, and I am not putting clients in a parking lot in parkas

### Problems:
- "Yep, we'll be there!" is not a confirmation — I needed date, arrival time, duration, truck and driver's cell in writing, and I had to chase and pin it down myself
- I lost sleep the night before the client event because the paper trail was thinner than my standards — the 22-person no-show lives in the back of my head every single time
- Allergen answers came slowly and informally; with an EpiPen allergy on the team, "let me check with the kitchen" is the difference between booking and walking to a competitor with it all spelled out
- The invoice needed a couple of asks and arrived a little informal — finance bounces anything that isn't an itemised PDF with GST broken out and our PO on it, and then I'm chasing again
- No stated guarantee that a booked order is reserved stock — the Saturday sellout replays in my head with a client watching
- The winter question is still open, which caps this at a spring-to-fall relationship unless someone gives me an indoor or drop-off answer

### Tasks:
- Chase and pin down the booking confirmation until it's forwardable to my VP
- E-mail for allergen details; escalate until the answer is written and specific; hand it to my colleague to judge for himself
- Get the total pre-approved with finance using the up-front price; obtain the itemised PDF invoice after
- Deliver the monthly team lunch, then the client event; debrief — on time? at headcount? order intact?
- Ask the operator directly about ring-fenced group stock and the winter option

### Sentiment:
Vindicated-tired

### Think/Feel Quote:
"They were fantastic on the day — I just shouldn't have had to build the paperwork myself to believe it."

### Alternate Paths:
- The allergen answer never firms up, and despite preferring the food I book the better-documented competitor for the client event — not a close call when it's someone's airway
- Brisket runs short mid-service at the monthly lunch and the last six people get substitutions — survivable for the team, unthinkable for the client event
- The operator, once asked, produces a proper written confirmation template immediately — and my anxiety drops a full notch for every booking after
- Finance rejects the informal invoice outright and reimbursement stalls, souring an otherwise perfect delivery

### Channels:
- E-mail with the Calgary franchisee (confirmations, allergen chase, invoice)
- Phone (pinning down details; driver's cell on the day)
- Invoice/AP system (pre-approval, PDF invoice, PO reference)
- The truck on-site (Friday treat, monthly lunch, client event delivery)
- Office Slack (team comms, day-of coordination)
- Internal debrief with my VP/execs

## Stage 5: Opting In

### Goals:
- Stop having to remember BorderBlend exists — have them arrive at the right seasonal moment with the right operational information
- Book constantly with confidence: real written confirmation and a day-before reminder as standard
- Become the champion who routes more business in, year-round, winter option included

### Narrative:
So here's the thing: I unsubscribe from everything. My inbox is a warzone. But when the catering list invitation came through, I signed up without blinking — a hundred percent yes — because it's the boring version, which is to say, the correct version. The catering menu with the allergen sheet attached, so I forward it to the team and finance in one shot. Real availability. "Spring catering's open, here's how to lock a date." And written confirmations with a day-before reminder baked into how they operate. I've already locked our Friday treats and the monthly lunches through fall, asked about the winter drop-off, and told two other office managers. Feed my team well and I will love you forever.

### Questions:
- Will the list stay operational — dates, allergens, availability, invoicing — or drift into "check out our new taco"?
- Does the day-before reminder actually arrive before my first big booking, or is it a promise on a signup page?
- What does the winter offer look like in practice — drop-off catering? An indoor setup? Priced how?
- Can repeat bookings get a fast lane — same team, same billing, one confirmation e-mail?
- When I refer the other office managers, does their experience match mine — because my name rides on that too

### Problems:
- One promotional-only e-mail and my trust in the channel wobbles — I subscribed to an operations feed, not a specials flyer, and my unsubscribe finger is famously quick
- The subscription only truly pays off if it's wired to real booking — if I still have to dig up the enquiry form each time, we're back to everything being on me to initiate
- November through March remains the open question on my calendar; without a firm winter answer, five months of my catering budget goes elsewhere
- A first missed day-before reminder would send me straight back to chase-and-pin-down mode, which is exactly the job the subscription exists to retire

### Tasks:
- Join the catering list; forward the menu-plus-allergen sheet to team and finance in one shot
- Lock the season's recurring dates — Friday treats, monthly lunches — through the operator, confirmations in writing
- Ask for and book the winter/indoor or drop-off option for November through March
- Tell people — the two office managers in our building, and anyone who asks; the organiser who trusts you becomes your best salesperson

### Sentiment:
Loyal-relieved

### Think/Feel Quote:
"Talk to me like I'm running an operation, because I am — give me the boring stuff and I'm your best customer."

### Alternate Paths:
- The newsletter turns promotional and I unsubscribe in four seconds — the bookings survive, but they're back to being work I initiate
- The winter answer never materialises, and BorderBlend stays a seasonal vendor while a year-round competitor takes my November-to-March budget
- The booking-plus-reminder system works flawlessly for a full quarter, and I move the client-event tier over too — the highest-stakes business I have to give
- A referred office manager gets the old chase-the-confirmation experience, and I quietly dial back my advocacy until the process catches up with the food

### Channels:
- E-mail newsletter / catering list (operational signal)
- E-mail with the Calgary franchisee (recurring bookings, winter planning)
- Forwarded confirmations and allergen sheets to VP, team, and finance
- Invoice/AP system (clean repeat invoicing)
- Office Slack (announcing lunches; fielding "can you book them again?")
- Word of mouth to other office managers (exec/peer recommendation, outbound this time)
