# Journey Start

Neither of you has heard of BorderBlend yet. Omar, you run time-boxed weekday lunches in the Toronto financial district — "the job is: feed me, don't cost me time, don't make me think" (BB-INT013-t4, INS-C04) — and two-to-four times a week you're the one grabbing lunch for colleagues (BB-INT013-t16). Grace, you feed a ~30-person Calgary office across Friday treats, monthly team lunches, and high-stakes client catering, and you will only stake your reputation on a vendor you have vetted yourself (BB-INT014-t4, BB-INT014-t22). BorderBlend enters both your worlds the way it wins with professionals — not through the feeds, but through a trusted human recommendation (INS-C01, INS-C02). **Brand goal:** convert two already-winnable professionals into subscribed relationships — a professional on "truck is here today" lunch alerts, and a coordinator on the catering list — extending a habit the food has already earned (BB-INT013-t28).

# Journey End

Omar, BorderBlend has stopped being "the truck I take my chances on" and become infrastructure — "part of how my workday runs. You don't churn out of infrastructure" (BB-INT013-t28). You're subscribed to the truck-day alert, you plan your midday around one notification (BB-INT013-t22, INS-C14), and you routinely bring in two-to-four colleagues' orders. Grace, you're on the catering list you'd "a hundred percent" subscribe to despite unsubscribing from everything else (BB-INT014-t26): forwardable menu with allergen info, real availability, seasonal prompts, hard written confirmations — and you're the champion actively routing business in, "first in line" when the operational version ships (BB-INT014-t28, BB-INT014-t32). **Brand's intended end point:** both of you subscribed — a repeat professional on lunch alerts and a repeat coordinator on the catering list — turning earned quality into a scaled, year-round, group-multiplied relationship (INS-C14).

# Stages: (5)

## 1. The Trusted Tip

### Short Description
Discovery is human and physical, not the feeds (INS-C01, INS-C02). Omar, a colleague walking back from a meeting near King says "the brisket one," points at the truck, "and that was basically the whole pitch" (BB-INT013-t6). Grace, you find everything through logistics: an engineer at a downtown offsite says "there's a truck that parks by the LRT, the brisket one, you have to" — and because you're the person who has to feed thirty people, your very next thoughts are "do they cater? Can they invoice? Are they going to actually show up?" (BB-INT014-t2). A brand this good in person earns the tip; the stage is about the tip landing.

### Main goals
**Omar's goals:**
- Register a credible new lunch option without spending any decision effort on it — surprise is the enemy, and a trusted colleague de-risks the first try (BB-INT013-t4, BB-INT013-t6, INS-C04)
- Know whether the truck sits within a viable walk of your desk and your calendar (BB-INT013-t10, INS-C09)

**Grace's goals:**
- Identify a vendor that could level up your team lunches beyond the "sad-tray-of-sandwiches" default (BB-INT014-t2, BB-INT014-t23 — cf. BB-INT013-t24)
- Establish whether BorderBlend is even a candidate for group work: do they cater, can they invoice, will they show up (BB-INT014-t2, INS-C04)

### Main tasks
**Omar's tasks:**
- Take the colleague's recommendation on board and clock the truck's location relative to the office (BB-INT013-t6)
- Mentally file it for a day when the calendar can absorb the risk of a line (BB-INT013-t6, BB-INT013-t10)

**Grace's tasks:**
- Note the tip and look BorderBlend up (borderblend.ca) with your organizer's checklist in hand (BB-INT014-t2)
- Plan a solo reconnaissance lunch before the team's name ever goes near it (BB-INT014-t4)

### Decision gates
- Is the recommendation trusted enough to spend one lunch slot on? (BB-INT013-t6, INS-C02)
- Omar: does the truck's location fit inside a between-meetings window? (BB-INT013-t10)
- Grace: does the brand pass a first logistics sniff test — is there any visible group/catering path worth investigating? (BB-INT014-t2)

## 2. The Solo Vet

### Short Description
Both personas try it alone first — and the hero product does its job (INS-C06). Omar, you stand in the line once, "reluctantly, because a line at 12:40 is a real risk to a 1 o'clock," and the brisket taco is "genuinely good. Like, good enough that I registered it, which food rarely does when I'm in work mode" (BB-INT013-t6). It's real — actually smoked — "eighty percent of the restaurant experience for twenty percent of the time cost" (BB-INT013-t8). Grace, your rule is absolute: "I will never put my name on something I haven't eaten myself" (BB-INT014-t4). You walk over on your own lunch, try the smoked brisket, and it's "not, you know, sad-tray-of-sandwiches good. It's actually good" (BB-INT014-t2).

### Main goals
**Omar's goals:**
- Verify the quality-to-time ratio in one visit: very good brisket in six minutes, not the best brisket in the city (BB-INT013-t8, INS-C06, INS-C04)
- Understand the failure modes you'd be signing up for — line length, and whether the truck is even there (BB-INT013-t10, INS-C09)

**Grace's goals:**
- Personally verify the food before any team exposure — vet first, always (BB-INT014-t4)
- Scout the operation up close: speed, service, how they handle a rush and a sellout (BB-INT014-t30)

### Main tasks
**Omar's tasks:**
- Walk down on a low-stakes day, queue once, order the smoked brisket taco (BB-INT013-t6)
- Time the full desk-to-desk loop and judge whether it fits between two meetings (BB-INT013-t8)

**Grace's tasks:**
- Take your own lunch hour for the trial run; order the brisket (BB-INT014-t2)
- Observe the truck as a vendor, not just a meal — throughput, professionalism, what sells out and when (BB-INT014-t30)
- Begin looking up group orders and catering as soon as the food clears the bar (BB-INT014-t2)

### Decision gates
- Did the food genuinely register — good enough to enter a professional's rotation? (BB-INT013-t6, INS-C06)
- Omar: does the time cost reliably fit the calendar, line included? (BB-INT013-t8, BB-INT013-t10)
- Grace: is the quality strong enough to justify the next step — investigating catering, invoicing, and reliability? (BB-INT014-t2, BB-INT014-t4)

## 3. Into the Rotation

### Short Description
The habit forms — "I didn't decide to become a regular. It happened" (BB-INT013-t6) — and the group behaviour starts. Omar, two-to-four times a week you're saying "I'll grab lunch, what does everyone want," taking four orders, tracking who wanted no salsa verde and who's the plant-based one (BB-INT013-t16, INS-C04). The frictions that surface here are obstacles to going further, not doubts about the food: is this specific truck open right now, where exactly, and how long is the line (BB-INT013-t12, INS-C09); an order-ahead app that "added uncertainty instead of removing it" (BB-INT013-t14, INS-C10). Grace, you find the catering path "kind of buried, honestly. It felt built for the person buying one taco on their lunch, not for me booking for thirty" (BB-INT014-t8) — and you start with the low-stakes Friday team treat before anything bigger (BB-INT014-t22).

### Main goals
**Omar's goals:**
- Solve lunch once and never re-solve it — make BorderBlend the default known quantity (BB-INT013-t4, INS-C04)
- Turn the grudging solo-run-for-four into a happy group order: four labelled items, one bag, one pickup (BB-INT013-t16, INS-C10)
- Answer the pre-departure question every time: open, where, how long is the line — "if I had those three data points on my phone I would never have a bad lunch again" (BB-INT013-t12, INS-C09)

**Grace's goals:**
- Move from personal endorsement to a first team order without putting your reputation at risk — start with the casual Friday treat, not the client event (BB-INT014-t22, INS-C04)
- Get the price up front, per head, so it can be pre-approved — "I'm more sensitive to surprises than to the number" (BB-INT014-t20, INS-C08)

### Main tasks
**Omar's tasks:**
- Fold the truck into the weekly rotation; collect and carry colleagues' orders, dietary quirks included (BB-INT013-t16)
- Try the app for order-ahead — the dream workflow: tap from the desk, walk down, one bag waiting (BB-INT013-t14, INS-C10)
- Expense each run; wrestle the crumpled thermal receipt that finance doesn't want to see (BB-INT013-t18)

**Grace's tasks:**
- Hunt down the catering/group front door on the website and submit the enquiry form (BB-INT014-t8)
- Work availability, menu, and headcount out with the local operator over email and a call (BB-INT014-t8)
- Send the team form: how many, and who can't eat what (BB-INT014-t14)

### Decision gates
- Omar: does the truck show up reliably enough — and does the line stay survivable — for BorderBlend to hold the rotation slot? (BB-INT013-t10, INS-C09)
- Omar: does order-ahead remove uncertainty or add it? If it adds, you fall back to walking up and taking your chances (BB-INT013-t14, INS-C10)
- Grace: can you get a date, a per-head price, and dietary answers solid enough to book the first group order? (BB-INT014-t8, BB-INT014-t20)

## 4. Proving the Operation

### Short Description
The stakes rise from lunch to reputation — and this is where BorderBlend, having already won on food, gets to win on operations. Grace, "reliability is the whole game... The food being good is table stakes... 'will you actually show up when you said, at the headcount I said' — that's the entire job" (BB-INT014-t12) — with a vendor no-show memory that "lives in the back of my head every single time" (BB-INT014-t6). You need the confirmation in writing — "I'm not forwarding 'yep!' to leadership" (BB-INT014-t10, INS-C14) — ingredient-level allergen answers for a genuine EpiPen allergy (BB-INT014-t14, INS-C11), and a proper itemized invoice, GST broken out, PO reference, as a PDF (BB-INT014-t18). Omar, you're the customer "waving money" at a catering option with no obvious front door — "I'm not asking them to convince me — I'm already convinced. I just need them to make it easy to say yes" (BB-INT013-t26, BB-INT013-t24).

### Main goals
**Omar's goals:**
- Expense cleanly, forty times a quarter: a proper itemized invoice, emailed, with a cost centre or client code (BB-INT013-t18, INS-C14)
- Find the front door for a defined, paid catering event — the working lunch that beats "the same three sad sandwich platters" (BB-INT013-t24, BB-INT013-t26)

**Grace's goals:**
- Secure a hard, forwardable, in-writing confirmation: date, arrival time, duration, truck, driver's cell — locked (BB-INT014-t10, INS-C14)
- Get ingredient-level allergen information in writing that your tree-nut/EpiPen colleague can judge for himself (BB-INT014-t14, INS-C11)
- Have group stock ring-fenced — reserved, prepped, guaranteed for your count, not first-come (BB-INT014-t30)
- Run the high-stakes client catering in front of executives — and know the winter plan, because a Calgary February needs an indoor or drop-off answer (BB-INT014-t22)

### Main tasks
**Omar's tasks:**
- Request the itemized emailed invoice; push the receipt problem toward a solution (BB-INT013-t18)
- Scope a team working-lunch or client-session catering order; work out who to even ask (BB-INT013-t24, BB-INT013-t26)

**Grace's tasks:**
- Chase and pin down the booking confirmation yourself until it's forwardable to your VP (BB-INT014-t10)
- Email for allergen details; escalate until the answer is written and specific, not "let me check with the kitchen" (BB-INT014-t14)
- Get the total pre-approved with finance using the up-front price; obtain the itemized PDF invoice after (BB-INT014-t18, BB-INT014-t20)
- Deliver the monthly team lunch, then the client event — and debrief on whether they arrived on time, at headcount (BB-INT014-t12, BB-INT014-t22)

### Decision gates
- Did the truck arrive on time, at the confirmed headcount, with the order intact? This gate is pass/fail for everything after (BB-INT014-t12)
- Were allergen and invoicing needs answered in writing — or does Grace book the better-documented competitor instead, despite preferring the food? (BB-INT014-t16, INS-C11)
- Did the client event land in front of the people Grace is trying to impress? (BB-INT014-t22, BB-INT014-t30)
- Omar: did a bookable catering path materialize for the money you're already waving? (BB-INT013-t26)

## 5. Opting In

### Short Description
The subscription moment — and for this persona pair it's earned with operational signal, not brand story (INS-C14). Omar, your inbox bar is "very high," but three things you'd open every time: "the truck is here today," actionable specials, and catering booking (BB-INT013-t20). The truck-day alert is "the highest-value message they could possibly send me and it costs them basically nothing... If I get a push at 11:30 that says 'Bay Street truck open till 2, brisket in stock' — I have just planned my entire midday around one notification" (BB-INT013-t22). Grace, you're normally "the person who unsubscribes from everything," but "for the group side? A hundred percent yes" — the catering menu with allergen info to forward in one shot, real availability and booking, seasonal prompts, and confirmations baked into how they operate (BB-INT014-t26, BB-INT014-t28, INS-C11). "Talk to me like I'm running an operation, because I am" (BB-INT014-t28).

### Main goals
**Omar's goals:**
- Never walk down to an empty corner again — plan the midday around one trustworthy notification (BB-INT013-t22, INS-C09, INS-C14)
- Graduate BorderBlend from "the truck I take my chances on" to load-bearing infrastructure (BB-INT013-t28)

**Grace's goals:**
- Stop having to remember BorderBlend exists — have them arrive at the right seasonal moment: "patio season's starting, here's how to lock a date" (BB-INT014-t24, INS-C14)
- Book constantly with confidence — real confirmation, day-before reminder — "and I'd stop stress-dreaming about lobbies" (BB-INT014-t26)
- Become the champion who routes more business in, year-round, winter option included (BB-INT014-t28, BB-INT014-t32)

### Main tasks
**Omar's tasks:**
- Subscribe to the lunch alert / loyalty list and allow the morning push (BB-INT013-t20, BB-INT013-t22)
- Act on the first alert — including trusting the honest bad news: "we're not out today" earns the good news more credibility (BB-INT013-t22)
- Keep converting the alert into group runs — two-to-four colleagues, several times a week, happily (BB-INT013-t16)

**Grace's tasks:**
- Join the catering list; forward the menu-plus-allergen sheet to team and finance in one shot (BB-INT014-t26, BB-INT014-t28)
- Lock the season's recurring dates — Friday treats, monthly lunches — and book the winter/indoor option for November through March (BB-INT014-t22, BB-INT014-t24)
- Tell people. The organizer who trusts you becomes your best salesperson — "first in line" (BB-INT014-t32)

### Decision gates
- Does the first alert prove accurate — open, located, stocked as stated? One false alert re-opens the uncertainty the subscription exists to close (BB-INT013-t22, INS-C09)
- Does the list stay pure operational signal? Brand story and recipes trigger the four-second unsubscribe (BB-INT013-t20, BB-INT014-t28, INS-C14)
- Grace: does subscribing connect to a real booking system with written confirmation and a day-before reminder — the feature that converts a subscriber into a constant booker? (BB-INT014-t26, INS-C14)
