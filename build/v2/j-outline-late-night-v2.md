# Journey Start:

You are Mateo — 26, working service and nightlife in Toronto, off your feet at 12:30 on a good night and 2:30 on a busy one. You know food; you can tell a real smoker from liquid smoke, and your scene trusts your calls. You have never heard of BorderBlend. Your late-night options are the usual suspects — a slice, a shawarma that's been spinning since noon — and you've quietly accepted that "open late" and "actually good" rarely overlap. The journey you're about to go on takes you from the first mention of a taco truck in your circle to becoming one of its most valuable people. **Brand goal:** BorderBlend — Canada's "leveled-up street food" challenger — wants you not just as a regular but as a subscribed, reachable superfan: on the newsletter / loyalty programme so it can tell you where the truck is parked tonight and hand you seasonal drops like Fuego Nights first, converting your organic word of mouth into a repeatable acquisition engine.

# Journey End:

You are a subscribed insider. You get the late-night location drop — "we're parked at Richmond & Portland till 3am tonight" — as a service, not a promo (BB-INT018-t26), plus early word on seasonal drops before the general public (BB-INT017-t24). The one thing that ever slowed you down — confirming the truck is out, late — is solved, and you repay it by showing up with the whole crew and posting every visit. BorderBlend now has a direct line to its highest-passion, highest-amplification customer at the exact hour demand peaks (INS-C13).

#Stages: (5)

## 1. The Word in the Scene

### Short Description
BorderBlend reaches you the only way anything reaches you: a trusted person in your world. "A friend… she'd been to the truck after a show and she wouldn't shut up about the brisket. And in my world, that's the real signal, tsé. Not an ad. Somebody in the scene saying *'non, but actually, go'*" (BB-INT018-t2). You check the Instagram and TikTok afterwards — close-up brisket, first-bite clips, steam — as confirmation that "makes it feel real" (BB-INT018-t4, INS-C01). Word of mouth opens the door; the feed keeps it open (INS-C02).

### Main goals:
- Find out whether this truck is worth your attention — you're food-literate and your recommendations carry weight, so you don't endorse casually.
- Confirm the hype visually before committing a late-night detour to it.
- Bank it as an option for the after-shift / after-venue ritual (INS-C03).

### Main tasks:
- Register the recommendation from a coworker or scene friend and probe it ("what did you get, was it actually smoked?").
- Look up the national and local BorderBlend accounts on Instagram/TikTok; watch the brisket and first-bite content (INS-C01).
- Cross-check the vibe: is this real craft or fusion-as-gimmick? (INS-C07)
- Note roughly where and when the truck shows up in your city.

### Decision gates:
- **Do I trust the source?** A scene-trusted voice saying "go" clears the bar; an ad would not (BB-INT018-t2).
- **Does the feed confirm it?** Content that reads as genuine craft earns a "next time I'm out, I'm trying it"; a dead or generic feed stalls the journey here.

## 2. First Bite at 1 A.M.

### Short Description
The conversion moment. A coworker walks you four blocks post-shift and there's the truck — string lights, smoke you smell from half a block out. "First bite I'm like — okay. Okay. What IS this" (BB-INT017-t2). You clock the smoke ring, the bark, brisket that "doesn't happen unless it actually sat in a smoker for hours. At a TACO TRUCK. At one in the morning" (BB-INT017-t4, INS-C06). Somebody who cares, still cooking at 1am — that's rare, and it's exactly the meal your eight-hour shift earned (BB-INT017-t6, INS-C03).

### Main goals:
- Cap the night with real food, not a regret — the earned post-shift meal (INS-C03, BB-INT017-t6).
- Test the food against your own standards: real smoke or faked, fusion that makes sense or a gimmick (INS-C06, INS-C07).
- Have a first experience good enough to justify telling your people.

### Main tasks:
- Follow a trusted friend to the truck the first time; order the smoked brisket hero.
- Apply the taste test: smoke ring, moisture, the salsa verde cutting the fat (BB-INT017-t4).
- Try across the menu — Korean chicken, the traditional core — to map what's worth evangelizing (BB-INT017-t18).
- Take the first photo: the unwrap, the steam, the truck lights (BB-INT017-t16).

### Decision gates:
- **Did it pass?** The first brisket taco either converts you on the spot or it's just another late-night option. "That was the conversion moment" (BB-INT017-t2).
- **Is the fusion earned?** "The flavours actually make sense… they earned the fusion" (BB-INT017-t18, BB-INT018-t20) — this credibility is your precondition for putting your name on it.

## 3. Running the Table

### Short Description
You go from convert to convener. The post-midnight run becomes "the after-party but it's tacos" — you roll up as a pack and you run the table: "you're getting the brisket, and you're getting the Korean chicken, and we're splitting so everyone tastes everything" (BB-INT017-t8, INS-C03). You've personally converted a dozen people — bartenders, servers, a DJ, a whole touring band — and you post nearly every visit, triggering a wave of "where is this?" replies: "I'm doing their marketing for free at 1am, bro — and happily" (BB-INT017-t16, INS-C01). You share it your way — "a story, the wrapper, the salsa verde, the corner we ended up on. My scene watches those" (BB-INT018-t22).

### Main goals:
- Be the one who runs the table — convene the crew and convert the sceptics on the spot (INS-C03, INS-C06).
- Champion fusion done right as a badge of taste (INS-C07, BB-INT018-t20).
- Build your own standing in the scene through the quality of your calls.

### Main tasks:
- Lead the post-shift group order; split everything so everyone tastes everything (BB-INT017-t8).
- Convert the purists and the sceptics — the carne-asada-only friend, the vegetarian coworker (BB-INT017-t18, BB-INT017-t20).
- Post the ritual shot every visit; field the "where do I get it" replies (BB-INT017-t16).
- Tag the truck — when the account feels alive; "if I look at their page and it's a graveyard, tagging feels like shouting into an empty room" (BB-INT018-t22).

### Decision gates:
- **Does BorderBlend keep earning the endorsement?** Consistent quality per visit keeps you vouching; your credibility rides on every recommendation.
- **Is the brand present on the other end?** A live, responsive local account keeps your amplification flowing; dormancy quietly throttles it (BB-INT018-t22, INS-C09).

## 4. The Hunt

### Short Description
You're all-in — and now the only thing between BorderBlend and even more of your money is *finding it*. The trucks move at night, to venues and the clubs on Richmond, "and you have no idea where. The app says 'open now' and then you get there and it's just... not there" (BB-INT017-t10, INS-C09). So you run an intelligence network — "guesswork and DMs, man… a little underground intelligence network just to find tacos" (BB-INT017-t12) — checking the app "the way you check a weather app you don't believe" (BB-INT017-t14, INS-C10) against a local Instagram whose last post is days old: "the silence reads as closed, even when you're not… you lose to certainty, not to quality" (BB-INT018-t10). The effort you spend here is proof of how good the food is — a superfan this determined is demand the brand has already won, waiting to be armed.

### Main goals:
- Confirm, at 1am, which truck is open and exactly where — before committing the walk (INS-C09).
- Get the crew there while the appetite and the night are still live.
- Never again end a night "mad and still hungry" on an empty corner (BB-INT017-t14).

### Main tasks:
- Check the app's "open now," then cross-reference the local Instagram (BB-INT017-t14).
- DM the local account "yo you open, where you at" and hope for an answer inside twenty minutes (BB-INT017-t12).
- Text the network — "you seen the truck?" — a person being more reliable than the app (BB-INT018-t12).
- Make the call: commit the twenty-minute walk on a maybe, or take the certain option elsewhere (BB-INT018-t10).

### Decision gates:
- **Can I confirm the truck tonight?** Confirmation = you arrive with a pack and post it; no confirmation = ready-to-spend intent goes to "the place we *know* is open" (BB-INT018-t10, INS-C09).
- **Am I ready to be told, instead of hunting?** Every failed hunt sharpens your appetite for a direct channel — the exact moment the brand's subscription offer becomes irresistible (INS-C13).

## 5. On the List

### Short Description
BorderBlend offers you the one thing you actually need, and you take it instantly. "If there were a thing that told me, late — *'the Mile End truck is parked on Saint-Viateur till 3'* — I would subscribe to that in a heartbeat. That solves my actual problem. That's not marketing, that's *service*" (BB-INT018-t26, INS-C13). What lands is the insider register — location drops first, early word on Fuego Nights, seasonal previews — "the creator-friend energy, not the punch-card energy" (BB-INT017-t24), in authentic fr-CA where that's your language (BB-INT018-t28, INS-C16). You sign up, and the brand's best amplifier is finally reachable at the exact hour the craving peaks.

### Main goals:
- Get late-night location certainty delivered to you — solve the hunt permanently (INS-C13, BB-INT017-t22).
- Be in the know: first to the drop, insider status over points (BB-INT017-t24, INS-C12).
- Never miss a moment like Fuego Nights again — get the heads-up and organize the whole night around it (BB-INT017-t26).

### Main tasks:
- Sign up for the newsletter / loyalty programme the moment the location-drop value is clear.
- Set the channel that fits your night: push, text, or email — "open now, parked HERE" (BB-INT017-t22).
- Choose language and locality preferences — your city's truck, in your language, done properly (BB-INT018-t28).
- Act on the first drops: rally the crew, show up, post it — closing the loop back into Stage 3 at higher volume.

### Decision gates:
- **Does the offer lead with service, not marketing?** Location drops and insider access win the opt-in; an opaque points scheme you "don't even know if I have an account" for does not (BB-INT017-t24, INS-C12).
- **Is the register right — and will it stay right?** A friend-in-the-scene voice, in genuine fr-CA where it matters, keeps you subscribed; "English-only. Instant." unsubscribe, and "three 'ORDER NOW' pushes a day" ends it regardless of language (BB-INT018-t30, INS-C16).
